Myth-busting

Wrong beliefs that coststudents thousands.

Wrong
"I won't qualify for financial aid"

The #1 myth that costs the most

Even if you work or think your family earns too much — you don't know until you apply. FAFSA is free and takes 30 minutes. $10,000+ per hour ROI.

Wrong
"My grades aren't good enough"

Aid is about need, not GPA

Financial aid eligibility is based on financial need, not academic performance. A 2.0 GPA keeps aid — but isn't needed to initially qualify.

Misleading
"Community college is free — I don't need aid"

Aid covers way more than tuition

Aid pays for books ($500–1,000/sem), transportation, tech, housing, food, childcare. Students still get $10,000+ even with free tuition.

Wrong
"I live alone so I'm independent"

FAFSA "independent" has a legal definition

You're independent if you're 24+, married, a veteran, a parent, homeless, or emancipated. Otherwise — ask about a dependency override.

Wrong
"I need 6 units to get Pell Grant"

You can get Pell with just 1 unit

Pell scales with enrollment. 3 units = 25% of max. Part-time students still get money — just a proportional amount.

Wrong
"Submitting my application means I'm done"

Submitting ≠ getting your money

You may need verification documents. Aid reviews your file, you accept, money disburses after census. Check email and portal every day.

Satisfactory Academic Progress (SAP)

Getting aid is one thing. Keeping it requires meeting three standards every semester.

GPA requirement
2.0 GPA
Overall GPA, not just one semester. A D average makes you ineligible.
Completion rate
67% of units
Take 12 units? Must pass at least 8. W, F, NP, I all count against you.
Time limit
150% max time

⚠ What hurts your SAP
W, F, NP, and I grades all count as attempted but not completed. It's often better to pass with a C than withdraw. If you lose eligibility due to illness, job loss, or crisis — you can appeal. Document your circumstances and contact Financial Aid immediately.

Before the 9 steps

Four rules thatchange everything.

01

Time Is Your Superpower

$100/month invested at 18 becomes $1.4M by 67. At 28, same money — $700K. A 10-year delay costs $700,000.

02

Only Borrow What You Need

FAFSA will offer loans even when grants cover everything. You don't have to accept them. Decline every loan dollar you don't need.

03

The FOO Is Not Linear

An emergency drops you from step 6 back to step 2. That's normal. Direction matters more than perfection.

04

Lifestyle Creep Is The Enemy

When income grows, your lifestyle wants to grow with it. Every dollar of lifestyle inflation stops compounding for 40 years.

Your Aid Package Is Your Starting Capital

Stacked correctly, Pell + Cal Grant B + FSEOG + SSCG + CCPG can total $16,000+ per year — money you never repay. Understand every line item before anything else.

The Refund Check Is Not a Bonus

After school deducts tuition, excess aid is refunded to you via BankMobile. This feels like "free money." It's not — it's your living expenses budget for the semester. Treat it like a paycheck that must last 4–5 months.

Never Accept Loans You Don't Need

FAFSA will offer loans even when grants cover everything. You do not have to accept them. Subsidized loans at 6.53% don't accrue interest while in school, but unsubsidized start compounding day one.

Set Up Your Refund Account ASAP

Don't wait until disbursement day. Set up BankMobile direct deposit early so you receive your money the moment it's released — usually around the census date.

You Can't Build Wealth on an Empty Stomach

49% of CC students are food insecure, 50% face housing challenges. Before any investing or debt payoff — secure your foundation. Using these programs is not a setback, it's smart.

Claim What's Yours

  • CalFresh (SNAP): Up to $292/month in groceries. Apply at getcalfresh.org.
  • Medi-Cal: Free health insurance — saves $200–500/month.
  • Campus food pantry: Free groceries, no documentation required.
  • Childcare subsidies: CCFS, CalWORKs, Head Start.

Then: Build Your $500 Safety Net

Open a High-Yield Savings Account separate from checking. This single step stops the credit card spiral before it starts.

Where to Open Your HYSA

SoFi, Ally, or Marcus — all free, no minimums, paying 4–5% APY. Physical separation from checking reduces temptation.

What Counts as Toxic Debt

Credit cards at 24.9% avg APR. BNPL apps (Klarna, Afterpay, Affirm) — 30%+ on late payments, now on all three credit bureau reports since 2023. Payday apps (Dave, Earnin) — 300%+ implied APR. Rent-to-own even worse.

The BNPL Trap Is Designed For You

"4 payments of $25" sounds harmless until you have 6 open plans and one missed paycheck cascades into late fees. Late on a $35 Klarna payment? 7 years on your credit report.

The Avalanche Method

List every toxic debt by interest rate. Pay minimums on everything except the highest-rate debt. Throw every extra dollar at it. Mathematically optimal.

Federal Student Loans Are Not Step 3

6.53% is a gray zone — don't panic-pay while carrying 25% credit card debt. High-rate first. Student loans are Step 9.

Build Credit the Right Way

Why Credit Matters Before You Want It To

Your credit score affects apartment applications, car insurance rates, future loan rates, and some employer background checks.

The Secured Card Strategy

Deposit $200–$300 as collateral. Use for one small recurring charge (Spotify at $11). Autopay full balance monthly. After 6–12 months, issuers upgrade and return your deposit. Discover Secured and Capital One Quicksilver Secured are student-friendly.

The Authorized User Fast-Track

If a parent has good payment history, ask to be added as an authorized user. Their history gets added to your report immediately — fastest legal way to build credit from zero.

  • Pay in FULL every month — carrying a balance doesn't help your score
  • Keep utilization under 10% ($300 limit = charge under $30)
  • Never miss a payment — it's 35% of your FICO score
  • Don't apply for multiple cards at once — each inquiry drops ~5 pts
  • Avoid store cards (Target, Amazon, Best Buy) — 29%+ APR traps

The Non-Negotiable Rule: Earned Income First

You can only contribute up to what you earned that year. Financial aid does NOT count as earned income. You need a job or gig income.

What Counts as Earned Income

  • W-2 jobs — campus job, retail, food service, childcare
  • Gig work — DoorDash, Uber Eats, Instacart, TaskRabbit
  • Freelance — tutoring, photography, graphic design, web work
  • Babysitting / odd jobs — counts even without a 1099 if reported

Where to Open — Free, No Minimums

Fidelity or Schwab — $0 minimums. Invest in a Target Date 2060 Fund or FXAIX/VOO (S&P 500, 0.015% fee). Set up recurring transfers.

Why Starting Now Is the Entire Game

$100/month from ages 18–22, then never again — grows to ~$200,000+ at age 65 at 7% return. Tax-free. Starting at 28 with same total gives ~$46,000.

Taxes
Handle Gig Income Taxes
Self-employment tax · Quarterly payments · Don't get blindsided in April
▾

Nobody Told You About This

DoorDash, Uber, freelance — nobody withholds taxes. Earn more than $400 from self-employment? You owe SE tax (15.3%) plus income tax.

The Simple Rule: 25–30%

Every time you get gig pay, move 25–30% into a separate "Taxes" savings account. Don't touch it.

Quarterly Estimated Payments

Expect to owe $1,000+? IRS expects quarterly payments (Apr 15, Jun 15, Sep 15, Jan 15). Pay via IRS Direct Pay — free, no account needed.

  • DoorDash/Uber: mileage deduction = $0.67/mile — log every mile in MileIQ
  • Freelance tools, subscriptions, software are deductible
  • File free: IRS Free File (under $79K), VITA campus sites
  • $400 self-employment threshold — above it, file Schedule SE

Size It Realistically

The "6 months" advice is for homeowners. For CC students on CalFresh and Medi-Cal, 2–3 months might be $1,500–$3,500. Achievable in one semester.

Calculate Your Number

Add up actual monthly fixed expenses: rent, utilities, transport, phone, food beyond CalFresh. Multiply by 2 or 3.

The Hard Rule

Never invest your emergency fund. It must be instantly accessible. If the market drops 30% the same week your car breaks down, you need cash now.

Now Accelerate

Steps 1–7 built the foundation. Aim to max Roth IRA ($7,000/yr = $583/mo). Surplus goes into a taxable brokerage with the same index funds.

What to Invest In — Keep It Simple

  • FXAIX or VOO — S&P 500 index. 0.015% expense ratio. Best default.
  • Target Date 2060 Fund — auto-rebalances as you age. Zero decisions.
  • VTI — Total US market. Slightly broader. Also excellent.

The CC Transfer Superpower

~$3,500/yr at CC vs $43,000/yr at a 4-year = $79,000 saved over 2 years. That money becomes capital you invest instead of debt compounding against you.

Mindset Shift

Financial aid can replace income so you work fewer hours and study more. Students working under 15–20 hrs/week have significantly higher retention and grades.

Kill All Remaining Debt
Student loans · Car loans · Enter your 30s completely clean

Life Gets Expensive After School

Rent deposits, car, insurance, relationships — every debt you carry directly competes with building your life. The goal is to arrive clean.

Student Loans

Federal at 6.53%. If Roth IRA earns 7%+, the math is roughly a coin flip. Do whichever keeps you most consistent.

Car Loans — The 20/3/8 Rule

20% down, no longer than 3 years, total cost under 8% of gross income. A $25K car on a 72-month loan at 7% = $29,400 — car worth ~$12,000 at payoff. Buy used with cash.

  • Avoid car loans above 5% APR — buy cheaper instead
  • SAVE plan caps payments at 5% of discretionary income
  • PSLF forgives federal loans after 10 years of public service
  • Private student loans are less flexible — prioritize over federal
